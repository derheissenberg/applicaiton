## Origin — corporate trying to move at startup speed

In 2017, OBI Group created OBI Next to prove that a €8.2B retail corporation could move at startup speed. The bet: take an established business and run a real startup experiment inside it, with the discipline to ship a real product to real customers within a short, defined window.

Stefan joined as Senior UX Designer to launch the bathroom planner (BadPlaner) — the digital service that would become the blueprint for OBI's complete transformation. The brief: 30 days from concept to MVP. A live product, not a slide deck.

## War Room — 30 days to MVP

The team set up in an empty loft close to the agency — assembling IKEA furniture while planning strategy. Stefan facilitated design thinking workshops with stakeholders across OBI, synthesizing customer pain points, market research, and business goals.

**The key insight:** customers needed inspiration first, not complex planning tools. Most bathroom-renovation tools failed because they assumed customers came with clear requirements. The data said the opposite — customers were looking for someone to show them what good looked like before they could imagine what they wanted.

By week two, concept and design were ready. Stefan was sitting with developers and product manager, coding frontend implementations at 2am. He did whatever removed blockers: UX/UI/HTML/CSS/JavaScript, legal content, measurement frameworks. The discipline: do what needs doing to ship, then prove it works with data.

**Day 30: launch.** Customers could browse and customize complete bathroom looks, schedule in-store consultations, and receive planning documents.

But the team didn't just ship and hope. They analyzed every click and recorded sessions from day one.

## Learning and adaptation — validation as the actual product

The team recruited participants who'd recently renovated bathrooms and ran moderated sessions combining task-based testing, five-second tests, and unscripted exploration. The research showed exactly where the experience broke down and what customers actually needed.

The team adapted immediately:
- Questions moved earlier in the journey
- Inspiration filters simplified to essentials
- New planning documents personalized based on selections
- Budget ranges recalibrated to match realistic project scopes

Version 2 launched just weeks later. The numbers validated everything — conversion rates improved and consultation appointment bookings increased. Each change was A/B tested so the team could see exactly what improved performance.

When OBI started directing organic traffic from their main website to the landing pages, the performance held strong.

## Scaling the playbook

The team was ready to scale. Stefan helped onboard new teams with everything they'd learned:
- 3D-configurator architecture
- Lead magnet strategy
- Remarketing campaigns
- Validation framework

The Kitchen Planner team had a proven blueprint. They didn't need to figure out what worked — they started from the validated approach and adapted it for their domain.

## Blueprint for digital innovation

What started as a 6-month pilot became OBI Group's methodology for digital future. It proved corporate could move at startup speed — and that proof changed everything.

Within 18 months, OBI Next launched the Kitchen Planner and the Garden Planner using the same methodology. The bathroom planner expanded from Austria to Germany and then across Europe, backed with major marketing campaigns. All three services now run 14 configurators serving 300+ stores.

Today, OBI Group generates €8.2 billion in revenue across 640+ stores in 10 countries. 50% of German customers visit the website before stores. Project experiences — not just product sales — serve 9+ million users and define the brand.

The transformation is still compounding.

## What this case study illustrates

- **30-day sprints work when the validation framework is built in.** Shipping fast isn't the point. Shipping fast *with measurement infrastructure* is what made the project a blueprint instead of an experiment.
- **The deliverable is the methodology, not the artifact.** The bathroom planner was the visible output. The real product was the playbook for how a €8B company builds digital innovation — and that playbook is what scaled to 14 configurators.
- **Embedded consultancy works when the consultant skips handoffs.** Stefan didn't just design the bathroom planner — he wrote frontend code, drafted legal content, and built the measurement framework. The barrier between "consultant who designs" and "team member who ships" disappeared.
- **Validation before scale.** OBI didn't extend the methodology to kitchen and garden planners because of belief — they extended it because the bathroom planner's data proved it worked. Evidence is what makes a blueprint reusable.
- **Inspiration before configuration.** The biggest design insight from the workshops was about user mindset, not user flow. Customers came to imagine, not to specify. The product was rebuilt around that fact.
