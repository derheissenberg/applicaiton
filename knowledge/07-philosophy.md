## The six rules, in Stefan's words

### 1. Beginner's mind. *Learn rules to break them.*

*"Every product deserves a fresh look, even the mature ones. I stay close to the beginner mindset that questions assumptions and finds the unobvious path forward."*

The clearest evidence: the US onboarding initiative at myDHLi. Years of stakeholder assumption said compliance was the bottleneck. Stefan's team mapped the actual process and found something different — redundant approval layers between departments, with compliance taking the blame. Questioning the inherited assumption is what unlocked the 10-12 day → 24-hour transformation and the $2.5B segment. Same pattern at Saloodo!: trust dynamics and payment norms in MEA needed UX adaptations, not translations. The default assumption would have been "translate the interface." The beginner's question was "what's actually different here?"

### 2. Follow your passion. *No fear. Make it count.*

*"Fifteen years in and I still chase the problems that pull at me. When the work matters to you, it shows up in every detail — tone, friction, delight, clarity."*

Evidence shows up in what Stefan keeps doing voluntarily. Six years into Head of Design at DHL, he still prototypes hands-on. He still connects the Figma MCP himself. He builds OnlyPN with a co-founder on weekends in React Native. He ships this chatbot in five days as an open-source artifact. None of that work is required by the job title. It's the work that pulls.

### 3. Create new ways. *Frameworks travel. Teams, markets, and products don't.*

*"Build the playbook that fits your context, not the one that won last quarter in someone else's war story."*

This shows up in how Stefan builds. At Saloodo! he was the founding designer with no playbook — the team wrote its own as it shipped. At myDHLi the research function didn't exist; he built it from zero rather than importing a template. The first scaled design system across DHL business units was built for DHL's specific governance reality, not lifted from another company's playbook. Even this chatbot rejects the conventional answer (RAG + vector DB) for what fits the actual problem (prompt-stuffing + caching for a tight knowledge base).

### 4. Do what you can. *Get things done. Momentum beats perfection.*

*"Ship, measure, refine. The best design leaders I know keep 40 small bets moving, not 3 grand ones stalled at handoff."*

Saloodo! shipped fast enough to scale a marketplace from a regional pilot to 50+ countries. The MEA expansion went from Dubai pilot to nine countries in six months. At myDHLi, the dual-track agile model is built around small bets shipping continuously — the on-prem GenAI Hub, the conversational AI in support, the automated quoting flows, the design system tokens, all moving in parallel rather than waiting for a grand release. Side projects follow the same pattern: ship OnlyPN to the stores, ship the chatbot in five days, see what holds up.

### 5. Use what you have. *Don't do it in perfection. Constraints are the brief.*

*"Existing systems, brand assets, and team skills are raw material for the next leap, not blockers to bypass."*

myDHLi runs on top of a sprawling ecosystem of services, APIs, and legacy systems — Stefan's team works with that complexity rather than around it. The first scaled design system was built on top of existing component libraries across business units, not as a greenfield rebuild. The chatbot uses €25/month of infrastructure rather than the €200/month an enthusiast might spec. Working with constraints, not against them, is the consistent move.

### 6. Build with care. *Good taste is a service, not a signature.*

*"Build with respect for the people who live with your output — users, engineers, the next designer."*

The clearest evidence is in how Stefan's teams operate. Stable retention over six years at DHL. Designers who own services end-to-end. Component libraries connected to Figma tokens so the next designer doesn't inherit a mess. Research wired into sprint cycles so users aren't an afterthought. The US onboarding rebuild didn't just optimize a flow — it gave country managers the self-service tools they actually needed to do their jobs. Care for the people downstream of the work is the through-line.

## What Stefan goes deep on

Stefan also publishes three areas of deep practice — the work he is pulled into beyond the job description:

### Building

From discovery to ship. Founding designer at Saloodo!, five ventures of his own, eight years of freelance practice. Stefan loves the full loop — discovery, design, build, validation, ship. When handoffs slow things down, he skips them. Design and code are now close enough together that focused designers can move beyond the status quo.

### Team building

Six years of leading distributed teams across EMEA. Clear ownership, fewer handoffs, people running their own tracks end-to-end. Stefan stays close enough to coach, far enough to let people own the outcome. The framework is what makes trust possible at scale.

### Evidence building

UX research from scratch. Stefan goes deep on how a business actually works — how revenue flows, where the friction lives, what each team really cares about. Empathy for users isn't separate from that — it's what you build on top of understanding the whole product. That's when UX metrics and business metrics start shaping strategy together.
